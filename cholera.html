Cholera

Cholera is an acute diarrhoeal infection caused by toxigenic Vibrio cholerae and can cause rapid dehydration.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

Cholera is an acute diarrhoeal infection caused by toxigenic Vibrio cholerae and can cause rapid dehydration.

Causes & risk factors

It spreads mainly through contaminated water or food, especially where sanitation and safe-water systems are inadequate.

Signs & symptoms

Sudden watery diarrhoea, vomiting, thirst, weakness and signs of dehydration can occur.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis may involve stool testing during outbreaks or when clinically indicated.

Management

Rapid rehydration is the key treatment. Antibiotics may be used in selected cases according to clinical and public-health guidance.

Prevention

Safe drinking water, sanitation, handwashing and safe food handling are central prevention measures.

When to seek urgent care

Severe dehydration, inability to drink, very little urine, confusion or collapse require emergency care.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
