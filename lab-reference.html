Laboratory Learning Lab

Build the habit of interpreting laboratory results by test, unit, reference range, pattern and clinical context.

TestWhat it broadly assessesUseful learning patternImportant caution
Full blood count (FBC/CBC)Red cells, white cells, platelets and related indicesThink in patterns: anaemia type, leukocyte changes, platelet abnormalities.Use the reporting laboratory's ranges and clinical context.
Blood glucoseCirculating glucoseInterpret according to whether the sample is fasting, random or part of another test.Do not label diabetes from an isolated number without appropriate diagnostic criteria.
Creatinine / eGFRKidney function estimationConsider kidney function trends and context rather than one number alone.eGFR equations have limitations and are not interpreted identically in every setting.
ALT/ASTMarkers associated with liver/cellular injuryPatterns matter; they do not by themselves identify one diagnosis.Interpret with other liver tests, history and examination.
UrinalysisMultiple urine characteristicsCan provide clues about infection, blood, protein, glucose and other processes.Dipstick findings require context and sometimes confirmatory testing.
Golden rule: Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, population and context. This page intentionally teaches interpretation principles rather than giving universal “normal” numbers.

Interpretation framework

1Check the test and unit
2Check the lab reference range
3Look at the clinical context
4Interpret patterns and trends
