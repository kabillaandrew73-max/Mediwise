Digestive Health

Constipation

Constipation commonly means stools that are infrequent, hard or difficult to pass.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

Constipation commonly means stools that are infrequent, hard or difficult to pass.

Causes & risk factors

Low dietary fibre, inadequate fluid intake, inactivity, some medicines and medical conditions can contribute.

Signs & symptoms

Hard stools, straining, bloating and a feeling of incomplete emptying are common.

Diagnosis

Assessment is usually based on history and examination; further testing depends on warning signs and duration.

Management

Lifestyle measures such as fibre, fluids and activity may help; medicines can be used when appropriate after considering the cause.

Prevention

Gradually increase fibre, stay hydrated, remain active and respond to the urge to pass stool.

When to seek urgent care

Blood in stool, unexplained weight loss, severe pain, vomiting or a new persistent change in bowel habit warrants medical review.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
