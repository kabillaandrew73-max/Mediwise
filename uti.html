Urinary Tract Infection

A urinary tract infection is an infection affecting parts of the urinary system, commonly caused by bacteria.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

A urinary tract infection is an infection affecting parts of the urinary system, commonly caused by bacteria.

Causes & risk factors

Bacteria can enter the urinary tract; risk varies with sex, age, pregnancy, urinary abnormalities and other factors.

Signs & symptoms

Burning during urination, frequent urination, urgency and lower abdominal discomfort are common. Fever or flank pain can suggest upper urinary involvement.

Diagnosis

Urine testing may be used depending on symptoms, age, pregnancy and severity.

Management

Treatment depends on the site of infection and patient factors; antibiotics may be prescribed when bacterial infection is likely or confirmed.

Prevention

Hydration, appropriate hygiene and clinical management of recurrent risk factors can help.

When to seek urgent care

Fever with flank pain, vomiting, confusion, pregnancy with concerning urinary symptoms or severe illness requires prompt assessment.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
