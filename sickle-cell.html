Sickle Cell Disease

Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disorder in which abnormal haemoglobin can cause red blood cells to become rigid and sickle-shaped.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disorder in which abnormal haemoglobin can cause red blood cells to become rigid and sickle-shaped.

Causes & risk factors

It is inherited when a person receives relevant haemoglobin gene variants from both parents.

Signs & symptoms

Anaemia, pain episodes, fatigue, jaundice and increased infection risk can occur, with severity varying widely.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis uses haemoglobin testing and other laboratory methods; newborn and carrier screening may be available.

Management

Management is individualized and can include vaccination, infection prevention, pain management, disease-modifying therapy and specialist care.

Prevention

Know genotype status, attend routine care, stay hydrated and follow individualized prevention plans.

When to seek urgent care

Severe pain, fever, breathing difficulty, neurological symptoms or marked weakness require urgent medical assessment.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
