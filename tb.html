Tuberculosis (TB)

Tuberculosis is an infectious disease caused by bacteria of the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex, most often affecting the lungs.

Learn • Verify • Empower
Why this matters: Understanding the basics can help people recognize risk, seek appropriate care and avoid common health misinformation.

1. What is it?

Tuberculosis is an infectious disease caused by bacteria of the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex, most often affecting the lungs.

2. Understanding it

Pulmonary TB can spread through the air when a person with infectious pulmonary disease coughs, speaks or sings. Not everyone infected develops active disease.

3. Signs and symptoms

A cough lasting weeks, fever, night sweats, weight loss and fatigue can occur. Symptoms vary and other conditions can look similar.

4. Diagnosis

Diagnosis depends on the condition and may require clinical assessment, appropriate laboratory tests or imaging. A symptom alone should not automatically establish a diagnosis.

5. Treatment and management

Testing may include molecular tests, sputum examination, chest imaging and other assessments depending on the case.

Important: This is educational information, not a personal diagnosis or prescription. Treatment decisions should be based on appropriate clinical assessment and current local guidance.

6. Prevention and healthy habits

TB requires a specific multi-drug treatment regimen prescribed and monitored through a TB programme or qualified clinician. Completing treatment is essential.

7. MediWise Fact Check

“TB is spread by sharing plates and cups.”

Early diagnosis, appropriate treatment, ventilation and public-health contact tracing help reduce transmission.

MISLEADING — pulmonary TB mainly spreads through the air, not ordinary sharing of utensils.

8. MediWise takeaway

TB is preventable and treatable when diagnosed and managed properly.
