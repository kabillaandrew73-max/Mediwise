Diarrhoea

Diarrhoea means passing loose or watery stools more frequently than usual and has many possible causes.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

Diarrhoea means passing loose or watery stools more frequently than usual and has many possible causes.

Causes & risk factors

Infections, contaminated food or water, medicines, food intolerance and some chronic conditions can cause diarrhoea.

Signs & symptoms

Loose stools, cramps, nausea and dehydration symptoms may occur.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is often clinical; testing may be needed for severe, persistent or outbreak-associated illness.

Management

Fluid and electrolyte replacement is central. Treatment of the underlying cause depends on the situation; antibiotics are not routinely appropriate for every case.

Prevention

Handwashing, safe food and water, sanitation and appropriate vaccination where available help prevent infectious diarrhoea.

When to seek urgent care

Blood in stool, severe dehydration, persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain, high fever or marked weakness require prompt assessment.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
