Heart Attack

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked, causing injury to the heart muscle.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked, causing injury to the heart muscle.

Causes & risk factors

Coronary artery disease and risk factors such as smoking, high blood pressure, diabetes and high cholesterol increase risk.

Signs & symptoms

Chest pressure or pain, breathlessness, sweating, nausea or pain spreading to the arm, back, neck or jaw can occur.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis may include ECG, cardiac biomarkers and clinical assessment.

Management

Emergency treatment restores blood flow and limits heart damage; long-term treatment targets the underlying cardiovascular risk.

Prevention

Avoid tobacco, manage blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes, maintain physical activity and follow evidence-based preventive care.

When to seek urgent care

Possible heart-attack symptoms require emergency medical attention rather than waiting for them to resolve.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
