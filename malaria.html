Malaria

What causes it, how it spreads, the symptoms to watch for, how it is tested and treated, and how to reduce your risk.

Learn • Verify • Empower
Quick facts: Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites and is mainly transmitted by infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. It is preventable and treatable, but severe disease can be life-threatening.

1. What is malaria?

Malaria is an infectious disease caused by parasites of the genus Plasmodium. People mainly acquire it through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.

2. What causes malaria?

Five Plasmodium species can cause malaria in humans. P. falciparum is especially important in Africa because it can progress rapidly to severe disease.

3. Common symptoms

  • Fever
  • Headache
  • Chills
  • Weakness or fatigue
  • Nausea or other symptoms depending on the person and severity

These symptoms are not specific to malaria. A fever should not automatically be assumed to be malaria.

4. How is malaria diagnosed?

WHO recommends parasite-based confirmation of suspected malaria with microscopy or a rapid diagnostic test when available. Testing helps distinguish malaria from other causes of fever.

5. When is malaria an emergency?

Severe disease can include impaired consciousness, repeated convulsions, difficulty breathing, severe weakness, dark or bloody urine, jaundice or abnormal bleeding. These signs require urgent medical care.

6. How is malaria treated?

Treatment depends on the parasite, severity, age or weight, pregnancy status and local treatment guidance. Artemisinin-based combination therapies are important treatments for P. falciparum malaria. In Ghana, national guidance includes ACT options such as artesunate-amodiaquine and artemether-lumefantrine for uncomplicated malaria.

Important: This article is educational and does not prescribe a dose or replace clinical assessment. Use current Ghanaian clinical guidance and professional care for treatment decisions.

7. How can malaria be prevented?

  • Sleep under an appropriate insecticide-treated mosquito net.
  • Use recommended mosquito-control measures.
  • Reduce mosquito exposure, especially when mosquitoes are active.
  • Follow malaria prevention programmes applicable to your area and risk group.
  • Seek testing and appropriate care promptly when malaria is suspected.

8. Malaria fact check

VERDICT: FALSE

“Every fever is malaria.”

Fever has many possible causes. Malaria is one possibility, so symptoms alone should not be treated as proof of malaria. Appropriate testing is important where available.

9. MediWise takeaway

Don't guess. Test when appropriate. Treat according to current clinical guidance.
