Dengue

Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection that can range from mild illness to severe disease.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection that can range from mild illness to severe disease.

Causes & risk factors

It is caused by dengue viruses and spread mainly by infected Aedes mosquitoes. Risk is linked to mosquito exposure and local transmission.

Signs & symptoms

Fever, headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle/joint pain, nausea and rash can occur. Warning signs may appear as fever settles.

Diagnosis

Assessment may include clinical examination and laboratory testing depending on illness stage and local practice.

Management

Care is mainly supportive for uncomplicated illness, with fluid management and symptom control chosen appropriately. Avoid medicines that increase bleeding risk unless a clinician advises them.

Prevention

Reduce mosquito bites, remove standing water, use repellents and follow local vector-control advice.

When to seek urgent care

Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, breathing difficulty, marked weakness or altered consciousness require urgent assessment.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
