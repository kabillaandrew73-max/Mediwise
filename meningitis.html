Infectious Diseases

Meningitis

Meningitis is inflammation of the membranes around the brain and spinal cord and can have infectious or non-infectious causes.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

Meningitis is inflammation of the membranes around the brain and spinal cord and can have infectious or non-infectious causes.

Causes & risk factors

Causes include bacteria, viruses and other pathogens; risk varies by age, exposure and health status.

Signs & symptoms

Fever, severe headache, neck stiffness, vomiting, light sensitivity, confusion or rash can occur.

Diagnosis

Urgent clinical assessment and investigations such as blood tests and cerebrospinal-fluid analysis may be needed.

Management

Treatment depends on the cause; suspected bacterial meningitis is a medical emergency requiring prompt treatment.

Prevention

Vaccination, outbreak control and infection-prevention measures reduce risk for several causes.

When to seek urgent care

Severe headache with fever, neck stiffness, confusion, seizures, collapse or a rapidly spreading non-blanching rash requires emergency care.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
