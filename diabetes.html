Diabetes

Diabetes is a chronic condition in which blood glucose is too high because the body does not produce enough insulin, does not use it effectively, or both.

Learn • Verify • Empower
Why this matters: Understanding the basics can help people recognize risk, seek appropriate care and avoid common health misinformation.

1. What is it?

Diabetes is a chronic condition in which blood glucose is too high because the body does not produce enough insulin, does not use it effectively, or both.

2. Understanding it

Major types include type 1, type 2 and gestational diabetes. Persistently high glucose can damage blood vessels, nerves, eyes and kidneys.

3. Signs and symptoms

Increased thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, fatigue and blurred vision can occur, but some people have few symptoms.

4. Diagnosis

Diagnosis depends on the condition and may require clinical assessment, appropriate laboratory tests or imaging. A symptom alone should not automatically establish a diagnosis.

5. Treatment and management

Diagnosis uses established blood-glucose testing criteria. Different tests may be used depending on the clinical situation.

Important: This is educational information, not a personal diagnosis or prescription. Treatment decisions should be based on appropriate clinical assessment and current local guidance.

6. Prevention and healthy habits

Management varies by diabetes type and individual needs. It can include nutrition, physical activity, monitoring and medicines or insulin when indicated.

7. MediWise Fact Check

“People with diabetes must completely stop eating carbohydrates.”

Healthy eating, physical activity, avoiding tobacco and maintaining a healthy weight can reduce type 2 diabetes risk, although not all diabetes is preventable.

MISLEADING — carbohydrate needs depend on the person, treatment and overall diet; blanket elimination is not a universal rule.

8. MediWise takeaway

Diabetes is manageable, but it needs informed, ongoing care.
