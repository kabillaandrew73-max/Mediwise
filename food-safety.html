Food Safety

Food safety means preventing food from becoming contaminated or unsafe to eat.

Learn • Verify • Empower
Why this matters: Understanding the basics can help people recognize risk, seek appropriate care and avoid common health misinformation.

1. What is it?

Food safety means preventing food from becoming contaminated or unsafe to eat.

2. Understanding it

Contamination can involve harmful microorganisms, chemicals or physical hazards. Unsafe food can cause illnesses ranging from mild diarrhoea to severe disease.

3. Signs and symptoms

Vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain and fever can occur with foodborne illness. Severe dehydration, blood in stool or persistent symptoms need medical attention.

4. Diagnosis

Diagnosis depends on the condition and may require clinical assessment, appropriate laboratory tests or imaging. A symptom alone should not automatically establish a diagnosis.

5. Treatment and management

Diagnosis depends on symptoms and, in selected cases, laboratory testing. Identifying the food source may be important during outbreaks.

Important: This is educational information, not a personal diagnosis or prescription. Treatment decisions should be based on appropriate clinical assessment and current local guidance.

6. Prevention and healthy habits

Treatment depends on the illness and severity. Rehydration is important for many diarrhoeal illnesses, while specific treatment is needed for some infections.

7. MediWise Fact Check

“If food smells normal, it is always safe.”

Keep food clean, separate raw and cooked foods, cook thoroughly, store food safely and use safe water.

FALSE — some dangerous contamination cannot be detected by smell or appearance.

8. MediWise takeaway

Food safety starts before the first bite.
