MediWise Verify

Don't just believe it. Verify it.

A health-claim learning centre designed to help readers distinguish evidence from repetition, opinion and misinformation.

FALSE

Antibiotics cure viral colds

Antibiotics act against bacteria, not viruses. Using them when they are not indicated can also contribute to antimicrobial resistance.

Evidence approach: compare the claim with authoritative guidance and high-quality evidence.

TRUE

High blood pressure can have no obvious symptoms

Hypertension can be present without noticeable symptoms, which is why measurement and appropriate assessment matter.

Evidence approach: compare the claim with authoritative guidance and high-quality evidence.

FALSE

Natural products are automatically safe

Natural does not mean risk-free. Products can have pharmacological effects, interactions and adverse effects.

Evidence approach: compare the claim with authoritative guidance and high-quality evidence.

FALSE

More medicine always means faster recovery

Treatment depends on the cause and the person. Taking unnecessary medicines can cause harm.

Evidence approach: compare the claim with authoritative guidance and high-quality evidence.

FALSE

A health claim on social media is evidence by itself

Popularity or repetition is not evidence. Check the source, study quality, date and whether guidance supports the claim.

Evidence approach: compare the claim with authoritative guidance and high-quality evidence.

Verification rule: Start with the exact claim, identify what evidence would support or refute it, check authoritative guidance and look for the date and quality of the evidence.
