Brain & Nervous System

Stroke

A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is blocked or when a blood vessel in the brain bleeds.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is blocked or when a blood vessel in the brain bleeds.

Causes & risk factors

Major risk factors include high blood pressure, smoking, diabetes, atrial fibrillation and other cardiovascular risks.

Signs & symptoms

Sudden facial weakness, arm weakness, speech difficulty, vision changes, severe dizziness or loss of balance can occur.

Diagnosis

Urgent brain imaging and other tests are used to distinguish the type of stroke and guide treatment.

Management

Stroke is an emergency. Treatment is time-sensitive and depends on whether the stroke is caused by a clot or bleeding.

Prevention

Control blood pressure and other risk factors, avoid tobacco and follow prescribed preventive treatment when indicated.

When to seek urgent care

Sudden weakness, facial droop, speech difficulty, severe imbalance or sudden neurological symptoms require emergency care immediately.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
