Anaemia

Anaemia occurs when the blood does not have enough healthy red blood cells or haemoglobin to meet the body's needs.

Learn • Verify • Empower
Why this matters: Understanding the basics can help people recognize risk, seek appropriate care and avoid common health misinformation.

1. What is it?

Anaemia occurs when the blood does not have enough healthy red blood cells or haemoglobin to meet the body's needs.

2. Understanding it

Causes include iron deficiency, blood loss, vitamin deficiencies, infections, chronic diseases and inherited conditions such as sickle-cell disease.

3. Signs and symptoms

Tiredness, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath and pale skin can occur, depending on severity and cause.

4. Diagnosis

Diagnosis depends on the condition and may require clinical assessment, appropriate laboratory tests or imaging. A symptom alone should not automatically establish a diagnosis.

5. Treatment and management

A blood test such as a full blood count can identify anaemia, but finding the cause may require further assessment.

Important: This is educational information, not a personal diagnosis or prescription. Treatment decisions should be based on appropriate clinical assessment and current local guidance.

6. Prevention and healthy habits

Treatment depends on the cause. Iron is useful for iron-deficiency anaemia, but taking iron without establishing the cause can be inappropriate.

7. MediWise Fact Check

“All anaemia is caused by iron deficiency.”

A varied diet, prevention of nutritional deficiencies and appropriate care during pregnancy can help reduce some forms of anaemia.

FALSE — anaemia has many possible causes.

8. MediWise takeaway

Treat the cause, not just the number.
