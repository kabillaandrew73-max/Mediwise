Hepatitis B

Hepatitis B is a viral infection that affects the liver and may be acute or become chronic.

Educational information only. Symptoms can overlap with many conditions; diagnosis and treatment should be guided by an appropriately qualified health professional.

What is it?

Hepatitis B is a viral infection that affects the liver and may be acute or become chronic.

Causes & risk factors

The virus can spread through infected blood and body fluids, including perinatal transmission and sexual exposure.

Signs & symptoms

Some people have no symptoms. Others may develop fatigue, nausea, abdominal discomfort, dark urine or jaundice.

Diagnosis

Blood tests are used to detect hepatitis B infection and determine infection status.

Management

Management depends on whether infection is acute or chronic and on liver health; chronic infection may require monitoring and antiviral therapy.

Prevention

Vaccination, safer sex, screened blood and prevention of exposure to infected blood help reduce transmission.

When to seek urgent care

Severe jaundice, confusion, bleeding or signs of acute liver failure require urgent medical assessment.

MediWise takeaway

Use symptoms as a reason to learn and seek appropriate assessment—not as a way to self-diagnose. Reliable health information should be checked against current guidance.
